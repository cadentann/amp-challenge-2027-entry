# Method description and abstract

## Abstract (draft for submission)

We enter the AMP Challenge 2027 with peptides generated by the publicly released **AMP-Prompt**
soft-prompt generator (AMP-Designer, source commit `07d455dd`, weights from Zenodo DOI
10.5281/zenodo.17018363, CC-BY-4.0), ranked by a **frozen consensus selector** over two
independent antimicrobial-activity predictors, APEX (11 strain heads) and ANIA (EC/PA/SA).

The selector, `CONSENSUS_FIXED`, ranks each candidate by its worst-case percentile against a fixed
996-member anchor across the two predictor families, breaking ties by predictor disagreement and
then by median rank. It was frozen before any of the comparisons reported here and never retuned.

The selection policy was chosen by prospectively registered experiments rather than by
inspection. A first registered test compared a 5,000-candidate pool against its own nested
1,000-candidate pool on two previously unused generator seeds; all four pre-declared conditions
passed. A second registered test (protocol frozen before any score was computed) then evaluated
the same generator and the same unchanged selector at **full search opportunity** — all 48,133
eligible library members — against six pre-declared promotion criteria covering breadth, ANIA
preservation, diversity, novelty and completeness. All six passed, and full-library selection was
adopted. Across seven independent seeds, and against equal-opportunity controls drawn from an
AMP-Diffusion library, the generator-plus-selector combination showed a replicated advantage in
predicted Gram-negative breadth.

The submitted library contains 50,000 unique, challenge-valid peptides from a single fixed seed
(42); the ranked top-100 is selected from every eligible member of that library. Generation is byte-reproducible on repeat execution, and the selection pipeline carries a
decision-stability certificate across macOS and Linux: predicted values differ at float32 rounding
scale (worst 2.96e-05 against a pre-registered 1e-4 log10 MIC budget) while every binary activity
label, every breadth numerator and all 24 tested selector orderings are identical.

Against 10,000 random draws of 100 peptides from the identical scored universe, the frozen
selector's portfolio sits 26 standard deviations above chance on predicted Gram-negative breadth
(0.4843 versus 0.0709 ± 0.0157) and above the best of those ten thousand draws by a factor of 3.4,
establishing that the selection step extracts real structure from the predictor outputs rather than
benefiting from a larger search. This is an internal check against the same predictors — not biological
or independent validation — and it says nothing about whether those predictors transfer to a measured
panel.

Two homology-controlled results bear on generalisation. Against the **MarLys** reference database
(103,143 known AMPs — the database the challenge proposal names), the submitted top-100 has a maximum
MMseqs2 identity of **68.7%** with zero peptides above the proposal's 80% limit **under MMseqs2's own
default coverage setting**; the proposal does not publish its parameters, and under a permissive
coverage threshold all compared portfolios including ours would fail. **None of the 50,000 library
peptides is an exact match to any known AMP**, which is parameter-free. Stratifying the scored universe by
identity to that database, the selector's advantage over a random draw *from the same stratum* is
largest (z = +16.9) among the 20,062 candidates with no detectable alignment to any known AMP, and
not one selected peptide comes from the ≥70%-identity strata although 2,129 such candidates were
available with the highest random-baseline activity of any stratum. The advantage is therefore not
family recognition.

We also measured our own predictors against real MIC data rather than relying on published
benchmarks. On 906 peptides with measured MIC held out of ANIA's training set, **both predictors have
R² at or below zero on log10 MIC** — so on that dataset our absolute µM figures are not calibrated,
and published figures for this model class are if anything optimistic. This is one audited set of
species and protocols, not a universal statement about the models. What survives is rank signal and
threshold classification, and there the two predictors prove complementary in exactly the way the
frozen selector combines them: APEX is a high-precision, near-zero-recall filter (precision
0.89–1.00 at the challenge's 16 µM criterion, 1.7–2.5× base rate), ANIA a calibrated, high-recall one
(recall 0.72–0.85). One caveat from the same analysis: APEX's *E. faecalis* head shows no rank signal
(Spearman −0.055), and it contributes to our MDR breadth figure.

**All results are computational predictions.** No wet-lab measurement of these peptides exists.
Safety, haemolysis and selectivity are **unknown** — the one haemolysis predictor available to us has
negative R² on peptides as novel as these and detects roughly one in nine truly haemolytic peptides,
so its clean result on our portfolio is uninformative rather than reassuring, and we make no safety
claim. A comparison against an unmodified potency-ranked AMP-Diffusion portfolio shows this entry
marginally behind on predicted Gram-negative breadth (−0.0071) and on mean predicted MIC, while ahead
on Gram-positive breadth, MDR breadth, both ANIA endpoints, and carrying roughly a third of that
portfolio's internal sequence redundancy. That comparator is also the challenge's own excluded
baseline, and it showed MarLys-rule violations under the coverage settings we tested where this entry
showed none — though the organizers' MMseqs2 parameters are unpublished, so that is a measurement
rather than a determination. Every comparison,
including the adverse ones and the metrics we found to be broken or circular, is reported in the
limitations.

## Method

### 1. Generation

- Model: released AMP-Prompt — GPT-2 LM head with a 10-token learned soft prompt, restored from
  `pytorch_model.bin` (SHA-256 `47944ff4…`, 174 tensors, all verified exactly at load).
- Decoder, frozen: `top_k=10`, `top_p=1.0`, `temperature=1.0`, `max_new_tokens=34`,
  `batch_size=128`. One RNG initialization per invocation; a continuous stream of 128-row batches.
- Runtime, frozen: Python 3.12.3, PyTorch 2.8.0+cu128, CUDA 12.8, Transformers 4.24.0, **no
  fast-tokenizers package**, `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`, TF32 disabled.
- Seed 42 (the challenge template's own default), 65,536 raw attempts.

### 2. Eligibility

Applied by unchanged frozen code. A sequence is **library-valid** if it parses, uses only the 20
canonical residues, is 8–50 residues, is not a duplicate, and is not an exact match to
`data/antibacterial.fasta`. It is additionally **top-eligible** if its similarity to that reference
set is at or below the 0.80 cap.

From 65,536 raw attempts: 63,403 library-valid, 61,073 top-eligible. Rejections: 2,330 reference
similarity, 2,124 alphabet/length, 6 exact reference matches, 3 duplicates.

### 3. Library

`library.fasta` is the first 50,000 library-valid sequences in generation order.

### 4. Selection universe

All **48,133** top-eligible members of the library are considered. Restricting the universe to
library members guarantees the top-100 is a subset of the library, as the official validator
requires. (An earlier frozen policy used a 5,000-member score-blind hash subset; it was superseded
by a prospectively frozen full-opportunity experiment that passed all six pre-declared criteria.)

### 5. Scoring and ranking

Each candidate is scored by APEX (8 ensemble members → 11 strain heads) and ANIA (EC/PA/SA),
CPU-only, batch 32, two compute threads, one interop thread. `CONSENSUS_FIXED` then ranks by
`(−min(apex_rank, ania_rank), disagreement, −median_rank, sequence_hash, sequence)` against the
fixed 996-member anchor. The top 100 become `top.fasta`.

### 6. What was frozen, and when

The selector, anchor, decoder settings and eligibility rules were fixed before any experiment that
chose the policy. Both policy decisions were made under gates written in advance: the four
scale-test conditions, and the six full-opportunity promotion criteria (protocol sha `9f69ec59…`,
frozen 2026-09-25T06:29:42Z before any score was computed).

Three post-hoc changes were made, none driven by an observed score: a hard-rule compliance fix
restricting the selection universe to library members, forced by the official validator; a `uv`
dependency override removing `tokenizers`, forced by `uv sync` failing on a clean machine —
reproducing the environment that was actually validated, in which no tokenizers package was
present; and the addition of `scripts/prepare_entry.py`, which retrieves and hash-verifies the
pinned third-party model weights that this repository does not redistribute. The last is packaging
only: it fetches the same pinned bytes the validated runs used, and the pipeline it feeds is
unchanged.
